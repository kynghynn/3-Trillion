01 / THE PLACEPapua New Guinea → Manus

A country of islands.
A connection to care.

Papua New Guinea lies in the south-west Pacific, north of Australia. It includes the eastern half of New Guinea and many surrounding islands.

Its capital is Port Moresby. Mountains, scattered islands and diverse local communities shape how public services reach people. Our proposal focuses on one island province: Manus.

02 / WHY THIS PROJECTMore than a transport problem

A boat alone
cannot fill a shelf.

A medicine shortage can begin long before a delivery is missed. Stock must be allocated, funds released, orders matched to demand and medicines received in usable condition.

The proposal connects these tasks. It targets transport and handover failures, ordering errors and quality losses, while identifying upstream supply and funding gaps early. It does not assume that shipping alone can solve national supply shortages.

01

Available stock

Confirm the designated supply route and allocated medicines before contracting regular deliveries.

02

Funds that arrive on time

A budget line is not cash in hand. Confirm funds for transport, fuel and the final handover.

03

An order grounded in need

Use stock records and consumption, correcting for days when a stockout prevented use.

04

Usable medicines, received

Protect cargo, check expiry and damage, and close the delivery with a facility receipt.

The intended change: fewer interrupted treatments and fewer days without essential medicines. Facility records and local assessment will establish the baseline; this is a proposal, not a record of results.

    05 / THE AMBITIONMeasure what matters

    Fewer days
    without+
    essential medicines.

    Success is measured by how much we reduce the days when health facilities have no essential medicines—not by the number of deliveries.

    PRIMARY OUTCOME

    Stockout-day rate
    for tracked essential medicines

    Facility–medicine–days with zero usable stock ÷ observed facility–medicine–days × 100. Count shortages from every cause, including upstream supply failures.

    The planning document proposes a 30% relative reduction from baseline. This is a provisional target to agree after the baseline assessment, not an achieved result.

    What does this mean in days per month?

    For each facility, also report monthly stockout days averaged across the fixed list of tracked medicines. Compare the same facilities and medicines over time. This helps explain the primary rate; missing records must not be treated as zero stockouts.

    01

    On-time delivery

    Handovers completed by the planned deadline. Report the original schedule and verified weather-related exceptions separately.

    02

    Stock accuracy

    Counted items whose usable physical stock exactly matches the stock ledger.

    03

    Complete handover

    Deliveries with quantity and quality checks, a named recipient and complete handover records. Agree the operational definition locally.

    04

    Quality losses

    Unusable stock due to damage, deterioration or expiry, reported by medicine and value—not by adding incompatible units.

    07 / PRINCIPLESLocal leadership. Clear responsibility.

    Nothing lasting
    is built alone.

    One shared outcome, distinct responsibilities. Public health decisions remain with the health authorities; navigation safety remains with the captain.

    01

    Manus Provincial Health Authority

    Lead facility and medicine selection, approve allocation and quality decisions, oversee performance and public data ownership.

    02

    Lorengau logistics hub

    Check stock, calculate replenishment, pack and dispatch medicines, and reconcile facility reports with receipt records.

    03

    Vessel operating team

    Provide qualified crew and suitable vessels, protect the cargo and document each voyage. The captain retains the final safety decision.

    04

    Outer health facilities

    Confirm quantities, expiry and damage on arrival. Store medicines safely and record usable stock, consumption and stockout days.

    Manus Essential Medicines Last-Mile Supply Chain Pilot

    An introduction to the team’s pre-feasibility working proposal, version 1.0, dated 20 October 2026. The proposal is the controlling source for operating assumptions; no implementation or funding is confirmed.

    Purpose & scope
    Reduce stockouts in outer health facilities through regular delivery, stock and ordering routines, quality checks and staff support. Start with three facilities, expanding to six only when safety, supply and funding allow.
    A 24-month pilot
    Four months of assessment and preparation, followed by 20 months of phased operation. Confirm facilities, routes, baseline records, supply agreements and usable operating funds before regular delivery.
    Medicine supply & the hub
    Use the designated public supply pathway. Track 20 approved essential medicine items and strengths, initially non-refrigerated. Lorengau checks stock, calculates replenishment, packs and dispatches.
    Transport & payment
    Compare existing shipping capacity with qualified local crewed charters. Two visits per month are a planning baseline, not a fixed 14-day promise. Verify voyage, fuel and receipt records for monthly payment; confirm available funds before departure.
    Performance & evidence
    The primary outcome is the stockout-day rate across fixed facility–medicine observations. A 30% relative reduction is a provisional planning target. Assess baseline, monthly reports, quarterly counts, a midterm review and a final evaluation.
    Planning budget
    PGK 2,356,810 for 24 months in the 20 October 2026 working draft. This is an estimate before local quotations; regular medicine purchases and existing public staff and facilities are separate. Do not treat it as secured funding or total health-service cost.
    Safety & continuity
    The captain makes the final sailing decision. Use suitable vessels, protected cargo, verified handovers and backup transport. Plan next-year funding, responsible staff and contracts before the pilot ends.
    What is outside the pilot
    No hospital ship, emergency patient transport, onboard library, large-scale new medicine procurement system or vessel construction. Partners, route contracts and funding remain unconfirmed.
